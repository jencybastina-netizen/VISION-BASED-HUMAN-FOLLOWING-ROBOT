Runs the same lighting-adaptive detector on your webcam. Click the person's shirt in the camera image to lock on. Works on https, localhost or a local file. Nothing is uploaded.
How it works
  1. Camera: a virtual pinhole camera renders what the robot sees (trees, walls and people can hide the target). Lighting effects (dusk, night, glare, dappled shadows, sensor noise) are applied to the pixels.
  2. Illumination normalisation: 2x2 averaging (denoise), auto-exposure, then HSV conversion.
  3. Appearance model: a hue histogram of the target's shirt is learned at start and slowly adapted. Back-projection gives a mask; morphology removes noise; connected components give candidates.
  4. Identity: candidates are compared with the model (Bhattacharyya similarity) and gated by the Kalman prediction, so a similar shirt is rejected.
  5. Distance/bearing: pinhole geometry from the box, fused with lidar range.
  6. Kalman filter (constant velocity) keeps predicting during occlusion. The robot walks to the predicted position and re-acquires the person when visible again; if lost too long it searches.
  7. Planner: follow-the-gap steering on inflated lidar ranges, so the robot goes around obstacles instead of into them.

Baseline mode uses a fixed red colour threshold, no filter, no prediction and only emergency stop. Switch between the two under the same lighting to see the difference.
